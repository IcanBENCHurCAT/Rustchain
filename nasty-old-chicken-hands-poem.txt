In the hick town shadows, where the factory's light
Fades to a crimson glow in the dead of night,
A figure moves slow, with a purpose grim,
Nasty Old Chicken Hands, a silent hymn.

His eyes, like coals in a dying fire,
Scan the streets, seeking his prey, desire.
And there she walks, Sara, with inked skin so bold,
Stories of rebellion, tales to be told.

But he sees not the art, not the lines so fine,
But the flesh beneath, where the darkness aligns.
With each step he takes, the tension grows,
A predator's patience, a hunter's woes.

His hands, like claws, though they never reach,
Leave a chill that makes her blood freeze.
He watches her dance, her laughter, her grace,
And in his mind, he claims her place.

The tattoos that cover her arms and her back,
Are maps to a world he can never attack.
For she's a woman of strength, of fire, of might,
While he's a shadow, a creature of night.

He follows her home, to the door she unlocks,
A silent guardian, in his own dark locks.
He waits in the dark, behind the old oak,
His presence a threat, his silence a cloak.

And when she turns, and her eyes meet his gaze,
He's gone, like a wisp, in the fading haze.
But she knows he's there, in the corners of her mind,
Nasty Old Chicken Hands, her silent, stalking kind.

For he doesn't want her love, or her touch,
He wants her fear, her every clutch.
To be the shadow that haunts her dreams,
The one who makes her heart scream.

And so he watches, with a cold, unyielding stare,
A creature of darkness, beyond compare.
Nasty Old Chicken Hands, in the hick town's embrace,
The ultimate stalker, in his own dark space.
